# Architecture Documentation

Produce architecture documents that are concise, traceable to requirements, and useful to engineers and stakeholders.

## Before writing

Inspect existing documentation and repository conventions. Identify audience, purpose, scope, system context, constraints, stakeholders, and required level of detail. Reuse established terminology.

## Document structure

Choose only sections relevant to the deliverable:
- Purpose, scope, context, and goals
- Stakeholders, actors, and key use cases
- Functional and non-functional requirements
- Constraints, assumptions, and decisions
- System context and container/component views
- Data flows, integrations, APIs, and event contracts
- Security, privacy, identity, and trust boundaries
- Deployment, infrastructure, and environments
- Reliability, observability, operations, and recovery
- Capacity, performance, and cost considerations
- Risks, open questions, and roadmap
- Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)

## Quality rules

Link design choices to requirements. Separate facts, decisions, assumptions, and open questions. Use diagrams where they clarify boundaries and flows. Keep terminology consistent across diagrams and prose. Do not fabricate measurements, commitments, or compliance claims.
